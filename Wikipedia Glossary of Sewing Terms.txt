<dl id="mwBQ"><dd id="mwBg"><i id="mwBw">This glossary contains terms used in sewing, tailoring and related crafts. For terms used in the creation or manufacturing of textiles, including spinning, knitting, weaving, and individual fabrics and finishing processes, see <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_textile_manufacturing" title="Glossary of textile manufacturing" id="mwCA">Glossary of textile manufacturing</a>.  For terms used in dyeing, see <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dyeing_terms" title="Glossary of dyeing terms" id="mwCQ">Glossary of dyeing terms</a>.</i></dd></dl>

<p id="mwCg"><b id="mwCw"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing" title="Sewing" id="mwDA">Sewing</a></b> is the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft" title="Craft" id="mwDQ">craft</a> of fastening or attaching objects using <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stitch_(textile_arts)" title="Stitch (textile arts)" id="mwDg">stitches</a> made with <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing_needle" title="Sewing needle" id="mwDw">needle</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn" title="Yarn" id="mwEA">thread</a>.  Sewing is one of the oldest of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_arts" title="Textile arts" id="mwEQ">textile arts</a>, arising in the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic" title="Paleolithic" id="mwEg">Paleolithic</a> Era.  Although usually associated with clothing and household <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linens" title="Linens" id="mwEw">linens</a>, sewing is used in a variety of crafts and industries, including <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoemaking" title="Shoemaking" id="mwFA">shoemaking</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upholstery" title="Upholstery" id="mwFQ">upholstery</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing" title="Sailing" id="mwFg">sailmaking</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookbinding" title="Bookbinding" id="mwFw">bookbinding</a> and the manufacturing of some kinds of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_equipment" title="Sports equipment" id="mwGA">sporting goods</a>.  Sewing is the fundamental process underlying a variety of textile arts and crafts, including <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery" title="Embroidery" id="mwGQ">embroidery</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapestry" title="Tapestry" id="mwGg">tapestry</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quilting" title="Quilting" id="mwGw">quilting</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appliqué" title="Appliqué" id="mwHA">appliqué</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patchwork" title="Patchwork" id="mwHQ">patchwork</a>.</p>

<dt id="baste"><dfn>baste</dfn></dt>
<dd>To <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tack_(sewing)" title="Tack (sewing)">baste</a> is to join fabric together with long removable stitches.</dd>

<dt id="bias"><dfn>bias</dfn></dt>
<dd><figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bias_(textile).png" class="mw-file-description"><img resource="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bias_(textile).png" src="//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Bias_%28textile%29.png/120px-Bias_%28textile%29.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail" decoding="async" loading="lazy" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/Bias_%28textile%29.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled 2x" data-file-width="211" data-file-height="160" data-file-type="bitmap" height="76" width="100" class="mw-file-element"/></a><figcaption><i>Bias</i></figcaption></figure>
The <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bias_(textile)" title="Bias (textile)" class="mw-redirect">bias</a> direction of a piece of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving" title="Weaving">woven</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">fabric</a>, usually referred to simply as "the bias", is at 45 degrees to its <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warp_(weaving)" title="Warp (weaving)" class="mw-redirect">warp</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weft" title="Weft" class="mw-redirect">weft</a> threads.  Every piece of woven fabric has two biases, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpendicular" title="Perpendicular">perpendicular</a> to each other.  Non-woven fabrics such as felt or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interfacing" title="Interfacing">interfacing</a> do not have a bias.</dd>

<dt id="bias_tape"><dfn>bias tape</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bias_tape" title="Bias tape">Bias tape</a> or <i>bias binding</i> is a narrow strip of fabric, cut on the bias. The strip's fibers, being at 45 degrees to the length of the strip, makes it stretchier as well as more fluid and more drapeable compared to a strip that is cut on grain. Many strips can be pieced together into a long "tape." The tape's width varies from about 1/2" to about 3" (10mm to 75mm)  depending on applications. Bias tape is used in making <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piping_(sewing)" title="Piping (sewing)">piping</a>, binding seams, finishing raw edges, etc. It is often used on the edges of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quilt" title="Quilt">quilts</a>, placemats, and bibs, around armhole and neckline edges instead of a facing, and as a simple strap or tie for casual bags or clothing. While bias tape can be handmade, it is also available in pre-packaged lengths (although usually only in basic colors).</dd>

<dt id="binding"><dfn>binding</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binding_(sewing)" title="Binding (sewing)">Binding</a> is used as both a noun and a verb to refer to <i>finishing</i> a seam, edge or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hem" title="Hem">hem</a> of a garment, usually by rolling or pressing then stitching on an edging or trim.</dd>
</dl>

</span><dl class="glossary " about="#mwt35" id="mwMQ">
<dt id="dart"><dfn>dart</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dart_(sewing)" title="Dart (sewing)">dart</a> is a common technique used for shaping garments. Darts are created by stitching out a wedge-shaped fold of fabric. They vary in width and length and can be tapered at one or both ends. They frequently appear around the bust and waist.</dd>

<dt id="darning"><dfn>darning</dfn></dt>
<dd>1.<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darning" title="Darning">Darning</a> is a technique for repairing holes or worn areas in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">fabric</a> or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knitting" title="Knitting">knitting</a> using <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing_needle" title="Sewing needle">needle</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn" title="Yarn">thread</a>. It is often done by hand, but it is also possible to darn with a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing_machine" title="Sewing machine">sewing machine</a>. Hand darning employs the darning stitch, a simple <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_stitch" title="Running stitch" class="mw-redirect">running stitch</a> in which the thread is "woven" in rows along the grain of the fabric, with the stitcher reversing direction at the end of each row, and then filling in the framework thus created, as if weaving.</dd>
<dd>2.<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span>Darning also refers to any of several needlework techniques that are worked using darning stitches, including <i>pattern darning</i> (a type of embroidery), <i>net darning</i> or filet lace, and <i>needle weaving</i>, a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drawn_thread_work" title="Drawn thread work">drawn thread work</a> technique.<sup about="#mwt42" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECaulfeildSaward1885_1-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTECaulfeildSaward1885"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTECaulfeildSaward1885-1"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECaulfeildSaward1885-1" id="mwMg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwMw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwNA">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwNQ">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

<dt id="darning_mushroom"><dfn>darning mushroom</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darning" title="Darning">darning mushroom</a> is a tool used for <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing" title="Sewing">darning</a> clothes, particularly <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sock" title="Sock">socks</a>. The sock can be stretched over the "cap" of the mushroom, and gathered tightly around the stalk to provide taut surface for darning.</dd>

<dt id="dressmaker"><dfn>dressmaker</dfn></dt>
<dd>1.<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dressmaker" title="Dressmaker">dressmaker</a> is a person who makes custom <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing" title="Clothing">clothing</a> for women, such as <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dress" title="Dress">dresses</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blouse" title="Blouse">blouses</a>, and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gown" title="Gown">evening gowns</a>.  Also called a <i>mantua-maker</i> (historically) or a <i>modiste</i>.</dd>
<dd>2.<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dressmaker" title="Dressmaker">Dressmaker</a> as an <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective" title="Adjective">adjective</a> denotes clothing made in the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion" title="Fashion">style</a> of a dressmaker, frequently in the term <i>dressmaker details</i> which includes <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruffle_(sewing)" title="Ruffle (sewing)">ruffles</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frill_(fashion)" title="Frill (fashion)" class="mw-redirect">frills</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbon" title="Ribbon">ribbon</a> or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braid" title="Braid">braid</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trim_(sewing)" title="Trim (sewing)">trim</a>.  <i>Dressmaker</i> in this sense is contrasted to <i><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailor" title="Tailor">tailored</a></i> and has fallen out of use since the rise of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casual_wear" title="Casual wear">casual wear</a> in the mid-twentieth century.</dd>

<dt id="embroidery"><dfn>embroidery</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery" title="Embroidery">Embroidery</a> is an <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient" title="Ancient" class="mw-redirect">ancient</a> variety of decorative <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needlework" title="Needlework">needlework</a> in which designs and pictures are created by <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing" title="Sewing">stitching</a> strands of some material on to a layer of another material. <dt id="eyelet"><dfn>eyelet</dfn></dt>

<dt id="face"><dfn>face</dfn></dt>
<dd>The "front" of a piece of fabric having a distinct front and back; same as <i>right side</i>.</dd>

<dt id="facing"><dfn>facing</dfn></dt>
<dd>A facing is fabric used to finish the raw edges of a garment such as at neckline and armhole.  <i>Shaped facings</i> are cut to match the edge they will face, and <i>bias facings</i> are strips of fabric cut on the bias or cross-grain and shaped to fit edge.<sup about="#mwt71" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-3" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-3"}}'><a href="#cite_note-3" id="mwRA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwRQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwRg">[</span>3<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwRw">]</span></span></a></sup><link rel="mw:PageProp/Category" href="./Category:All_articles_with_incomplete_citations"/><link rel="mw:PageProp/Category" href="./Category:Articles_with_incomplete_citations_from_October_2019"/><sup class="noprint Inline-Template " style="white-space:nowrap;"><span typeof="mw:Entity">[</span><i><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources#What_information_to_include" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources"><span title="A complete citation is needed. (October 2019)">full citation needed</span></a></i><span typeof="mw:Entity">]</span></sup></dd>

<dt id="fusible_interfacing"><dfn>fusible interfacing</dfn></dt>
<dd>Commonly referred to as simply "Fusible" it is an <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interfacing" title="Interfacing">interfacing</a> fabric with glue on one or both sides that is ironed onto either a lining, facing or body fabric to provide structure.</dd>

<dt id="gather"><dfn>gather</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gather_(sewing)" title="Gather (sewing)">Gathering</a> is a technique for shortening the length of a strip of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">fabric</a> so that the longer piece can be attached to a shorter piece. It is commonly used in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing" title="Clothing">clothing</a> to manage fullness, as when a full <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeve" title="Sleeve">sleeve</a> is attached to the armscye or cuff of a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dress_shirt" title="Dress shirt">shirt</a>, or when a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skirt" title="Skirt">skirt</a> is attached to a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodice" title="Bodice">bodice</a>.

In simple gathering, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_(geometry)" title="Parallel (geometry)">parallel</a> rows of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_stitch" title="Running stitch" class="mw-redirect">running stitches</a> are sewn along one edge of the fabric to be gathered.  The stitching threads are then pulled or "drawn up" so that the fabric forms small folds along the threads.  Multiple rows of gathering are called <i>shirring</i>.<sup about="#mwt82" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECaulfeildSaward1885219_4-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTECaulfeildSaward1885219"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTECaulfeildSaward1885219-4"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECaulfeildSaward1885219-4" id="mwTQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwTg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwTw">[</span>4<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwUA">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt84" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPicken1957147_5-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEPicken1957147"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957147-5"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957147-5" id="mwUQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwUg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwUw">[</span>5<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwVA">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

<dt id="godet"><dfn>godet</dfn></dt>
<dd><figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Godet-dress.png" class="mw-file-description"><img resource="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Godet-dress.png" src="//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Godet-dress.png/120px-Godet-dress.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail" decoding="async" loading="lazy" srcset="//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Godet-dress.png/250px-Godet-dress.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail 2x" data-file-width="335" data-file-height="396" data-file-type="bitmap" height="118" width="100" class="mw-file-element"/></a><figcaption>Six-<i>gore</i> skirt with <i>godets</i>.</figcaption></figure>
A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godet_(sewing)" title="Godet (sewing)">godet</a> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ɡ/: 'g' in 'guy'">ɡ</span><span title="/oʊ/: 'o' in 'code'">oʊ</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="'d' in 'dye'">d</span><span title="/eɪ/: 'a' in 'face'">eɪ</span></span>/</a></span></span> or <span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ɡ/: 'g' in 'guy'">ɡ</span><span title="/oʊ/: 'o' in 'code'">oʊ</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="'d' in 'dye'">d</span><span title="/ɛ/: 'e' in 'dress'">ɛ</span><span title="'t' in 'tie'">t</span></span>/</a></span></span>) is a piece of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">fabric</a> wider at the bottom than at the top, often a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_sector" title="Circular sector">circular sector</a>, inserted into a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing" title="Clothing">garment</a> to add fullness for ease of movement or as a design feature.  Usually found in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeve" title="Sleeve">sleeves</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skirt" title="Skirt">skirts</a>, but also in very full <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell-bottom" title="Bell-bottom" class="mw-redirect">bell-bottom</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trouser" title="Trouser" class="mw-redirect">trousers</a>.<sup about="#mwt88" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPicken1957151_6-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEPicken1957151"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957151-6"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957151-6" id="mwVQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwVg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwVw">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwWA">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt90" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWolff199681_7-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEWolff199681"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEWolff199681-7"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWolff199681-7" id="mwWQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwWg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwWw">[</span>7<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwXA">]</span></span></a></sup> Compare gusset.</dd>

<dt id="gore"><dfn>gore</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gore_(segment)" title="Gore (segment)">gore</a> is a shaped segment, narrow at the top and wider at the base, extending from the waistline to the hem of a skirt.  Flared skirts can be made of 2 or more gores.<sup about="#mwt94" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPicken1957152_8-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEPicken1957152"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957152-8"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957152-8" id="mwXQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwXg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwXw">[</span>8<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwYA">]</span></span></a></sup> Four-. six-. and eight-gore skirts are common.</dd>

<dt id="grain"><dfn>grain</dfn></dt>
<dd>1.<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span>The <i>lengthwise</i> and <i>crosswise grain</i> of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">fabric</a> refer to the directions parallel to the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warp_(weaving)" title="Warp (weaving)" class="mw-redirect">warp</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weft" title="Weft" class="mw-redirect">weft</a>, respectively.</dd>
<dd>2.<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><i>With the grain</i> indicates parallel to the threads of a woven fabric, lengthwise or crosswise.</dd>
<dd>3.<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><i>Dyed in the grain</i> refers to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyeing" title="Dyeing">dyeing</a> with <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermes_(dye)" title="Kermes (dye)">kermes</a>, a red insect dye.<sup about="#mwt101" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-9" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-9"}}'><a href="#cite_note-9" id="mwYQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwYg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwYw">[</span>9<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwZA">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

<dt id="gusset"><dfn>gusset</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusset" title="Gusset">gusset</a> is a triangular or square piece of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textiles" title="Textiles" class="mw-redirect">fabric</a> inserted into a seam to add breadth or reduce stress from tight-fitting clothing. </span><dl class="glossary " about="#mwt213" id="mwvA">
<dt id="pad_stitching"><dfn>pad stitching</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pad_stitch" title="Pad stitch">Pad stitching</a> is used to secure two pieces of fabric together with perpendicular stitches. Pad stitching is mainly used for lapels and collars to maintain their shape.</dd>

<dt id="pattern"><dfn>pattern</dfn></dt>
<dd>In <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing" title="Sewing">sewing</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_design" title="Fashion design">fashion design</a>, a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_(sewing)" title="Pattern (sewing)">pattern</a> is an original <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing" title="Clothing">garment</a> from which other garments of a similar style are copied, or the paper or cardboard <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stencil" title="Stencil">templates</a> from which the parts of a garment are traced onto <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">fabric</a> before cutting out and assembling (sometimes called <b>paper patterns</b>).

Home sewing patterns are generally printed on <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_paper" title="Tissue paper">tissue paper</a> and sold in packets containing sewing instructions and suggestions for fabric and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trim_(sewing)" title="Trim (sewing)">trim</a>.</dd>

<dt id="piecing"><dfn>piecing</dfn></dt>
<dd>Assembling a piece of fabric, or a garment, by stitching together smaller pieces of fabric into a single whole. Commonly used in quilting.<sup about="#mwt224" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-21" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-21"}}'><a href="#cite_note-21" id="mwvQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwvg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwvw">[</span>21<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwwA">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt229" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-22" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-22"}}'><a href="#cite_note-22" id="mwwQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwwg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwww">[</span>22<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwxA">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

<dt id="piping"><dfn>piping</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piping_(sewing)" title="Piping (sewing)">Piping</a> is a type of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trim_(sewing)" title="Trim (sewing)">trim</a> or embellishment consisting of a strip of folded <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">fabric</a> inserted into a seam to define the edges or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_line" title="Style line">style lines</a> of a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing" title="Clothing">garment</a> or other textile object. Usually the fabric strip is cut on the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bias_(textile)" title="Bias (textile)" class="mw-redirect">bias or cross-grain</a>, and often it is folded over a cord. It may be made from either self-fabric (the same fabric as the object to be ornamented) or contrasting fabric, or of leather.<sup about="#mwt236" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-23" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-23"}}'><a href="#cite_note-23" id="mwxQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwxg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwxw">[</span>23<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwyA">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

<dt id="placket"><dfn>placket</dfn></dt>
<dd>1.<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placket" title="Placket">placket</a> is an opening in the upper part of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trousers" title="Trousers">trousers</a> or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skirt" title="Skirt">skirts</a>, or at the neck or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeve" title="Sleeve">sleeve</a> of a garment Plackets allow clothing to be put on or removed easily.<sup about="#mwt242" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-Picken254_24-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"Picken254"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-Picken254-24"}}'><a href="#cite_note-Picken254-24" id="mwyQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwyg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwyw">[</span>24<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwzA">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>
<dd>2.<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomacher" title="Stomacher">stomacher</a>.  Also spelled <i>placard</i>.</dd>
<dd>3.<span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span><span typeof="mw:Entity"> </span>A slit to allow access to a hanging <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket" title="Pocket">pocket</a>, or a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petticoat" title="Petticoat">petticoat</a> or skirt pocket.<sup about="#mwt246" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-Picken254_24-1" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"Picken254"}}'><a href="#cite_note-Picken254-24" id="mwzQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwzg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwzw">[</span>24<span class="cite-bracket" id="mw0A">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

<dt id="pleat"><dfn>pleat</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleat" title="Pleat">pleat</a> (older <i>plait</i>) is a type of <a rel="mw:WikiLink/Interwiki" href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/fold" title="wikt:fold" class="extiw">fold</a> formed by doubling <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">fabric</a> back upon itself and securing it in place.  It is commonly used in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing" title="Clothing">clothing</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upholstery" title="Upholstery">upholstery</a> to gather a wide piece of fabric to a narrower circumference.<sup about="#mwt250" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPicken1957256–257_25-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEPicken1957256–257"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957256–257-25"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957256–257-25" id="mw0Q"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mw0g"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mw0w">[</span>25<span class="cite-bracket" id="mw1A">]</span></span></a></sup>

Pleats are categorized as <i>pressed</i>, that is, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironing" title="Ironing">ironed</a> or otherwise heat-set into a sharp crease, or <i>unpressed</i>, falling in soft rounded folds.  Pleats may also be partially sewn flat and allowed to fall open below.</dd>

<dt id="pocket"><dfn>pocket</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket" title="Pocket">pocket</a> is a bag- or envelope-like receptacle either fastened to or inserted in an article of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing" title="Clothing">clothing</a> to hold small items.  In older usage, a pocket was a separate small bag or pouch.<sup about="#mwt254" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPicken1957258_26-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEPicken1957258"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957258-26"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957258-26" id="mw1Q"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mw1g"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mw1w">[</span>26<span class="cite-bracket" id="mw2A">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

</dl>

<dt id="right_side"><dfn>right side</dfn></dt>
<dd>The "front" of a piece of fabric having a distinct front and back; same as <i>face</i>. Sometimes called the "public" side.</dd>

<dt id="ruching"><dfn>ruching</dfn></dt>

This stitch can be used to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tack_(sewing)" title="Tack (sewing)">baste</a> fabric pieces together.</dd>

<dt id="seam"><dfn>seam</dfn></dt> or <dt id="seamline"><dfn>seamline</dfn></dt>
<dd><figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Parts_of_a_plain_seam.png" class="mw-file-description"><img resource="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Parts_of_a_plain_seam.png" src="//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Parts_of_a_plain_seam.png/250px-Parts_of_a_plain_seam.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail" decoding="async" loading="lazy" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/05/Parts_of_a_plain_seam.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled 2x" data-file-width="327" data-file-height="363" data-file-type="bitmap" height="278" width="250" class="mw-file-element"/></a><figcaption>Plain seam</figcaption></figure>
A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seam_(sewing)" title="Seam (sewing)">seam</a> or seamline in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing" title="Sewing">sewing</a> is the line where two pieces of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">fabric</a> are held together by <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn" title="Yarn">thread</a>.</dd>

<dt id="seam_allowance"><dfn>seam allowance</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seam_allowance" title="Seam allowance">seam allowance</a> is the area between the edge of fabric and the stitching line on two (or more) pieces of material being stitched together. Seam allowances can range from 1/4 inch wide (6.35 mm) to as much as several inches. Commercial patterns for home sewers have seam allowances ranging from 1/4 inch to 5/8 inch.</dd>

<dt id="seam_ripper"><dfn>seam ripper</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seam_ripper" title="Seam ripper">seam ripper</a> is a small <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool" title="Tool">tool</a> used for unpicking or cutting <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stitch_(textile_arts)" title="Stitch (textile arts)">stitches</a>.</dd>

<dt id="seamstress"><dfn>seamstress</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dressmaker" title="Dressmaker">seamstress</a> is a woman who sews and finishes garments, as contrasted with a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dressmaker" title="Dressmaker">dressmaker</a>.<sup about="#mwt289" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPicken1957289_27-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEPicken1957289"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957289-27"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957289-27" id="mw6A"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mw6Q"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mw6g">[</span>27<span class="cite-bracket" id="mw6w">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

<dt id="selvage"><dfn>selvage</dfn></dt>
<dt id="selvedge"><dfn>selvedge</dfn></dt>
<dd>The <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selvage" title="Selvage">selvage</a> (US English) or <i>selvedge</i> (British English) is the term for the self-finished edges of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">fabric</a>. In <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving" title="Weaving">woven</a> fabric, selvages are the edges that run parallel to the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warp_(weaving)" title="Warp (weaving)" class="mw-redirect">warp</a>, and are created by the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weft" title="Weft" class="mw-redirect">weft</a> thread looping back at the end of each row.  The selvage of commercially produced fabrics is often cut away and discarded.<sup about="#mwt294" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPicken1957289_27-1" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEPicken1957289"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957289-27"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957289-27" id="mw7A"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mw7Q"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mw7g">[</span>27<span class="cite-bracket" id="mw7w">]</span></span></a></sup> Historically, garments were frequently constructed of full loom-widths of fabric joined selvage-to-selvage to avoid waste.

In <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knit" title="Knit" class="mw-redirect">knitted</a> fabrics, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selvage_(knitting)" title="Selvage (knitting)">selvages</a> are the unfinished yet structurally sound edges that were neither <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casting_on" title="Casting on" class="mw-redirect">cast on</a> nor <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binding_off" title="Binding off">bound off</a>.<sup about="#mwt298" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-knit_idiot_28-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"knit idiot"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-knit_idiot-28"}}'><a href="#cite_note-knit_idiot-28" id="mw8A"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mw8Q"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mw8g">[</span>28<span class="cite-bracket" id="mw8w">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

<dt id="serging"><dfn>serging</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serging" title="Serging" class="mw-redirect">Serging</a> is the binding-off of an edge of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloth" title="Cloth" class="mw-redirect">cloth</a>.</dd>

<dt id="sewing"><dfn>sewing</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing" title="Sewing">Sewing</a> is an ancient craft involving the stitching of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloth" title="Cloth" class="mw-redirect">cloth</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather" title="Leather">leather</a>, animal skins, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur" title="Fur">furs</a>, or other materials, using <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing_needle" title="Sewing needle">needle</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn" title="Yarn">thread</a>.  Its use is nearly universal among human populations and dates back to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic" title="Paleolithic">Paleolithic</a> times (30,000 BC).  Sewing predates the weaving of cloth.</dd>

<dt id="sewing_circle"><dfn>sewing circle</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing_circle" title="Sewing circle">sewing circle</a> is a group of people, usually women, who meet and work on sewing projects together.</dd>

<dt id="sloper"><dfn>sloper</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_(sewing)" title="Pattern (sewing)">sloper</a>  is a base pattern used to develop other patterns. Often called a Block or Master Pattern. This pattern is highly developed and very accurate pattern that is designed to fit a specific set of measurements. This pattern is used in turn to create other more stylized patterns.<sup about="#mwt309" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKopp_et_al.199225_29-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEKopp et al.199225"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEKopp_et_al.199225-29"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKopp_et_al.199225-29" id="mw9A"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mw9Q"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mw9g">[</span>29<span class="cite-bracket" id="mw9w">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

<dt id="staystitching"><dfn>staystitching</dfn></dt>
<dd>A  stay stitch is a stitch that is used inside the seam allowance to stop the fabric from stretching.<sup about="#mwt315" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-30" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-30"}}'><a href="#cite_note-30" id="mw-A"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mw-Q"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mw-g">[</span>30<span class="cite-bracket" id="mw-w">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

<dt id="stitch"><dfn>stitch</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stitch_(textile_arts)" title="Stitch (textile arts)">stitch</a> is a single turn or loop of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn" title="Yarn">thread or yarn</a> in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing" title="Sewing">sewing</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knitting" title="Knitting">knitting</a>, and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery" title="Embroidery">embroidery</a>. All stitches made with a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing_needle" title="Sewing needle">sewing needle</a> with an "eye" or hole are variations on seven basic stitches: <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_stitch" title="Running stitch" class="mw-redirect">running stitch</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backstitch" title="Backstitch">backstitch</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overcast_stitch" title="Overcast stitch">overcast stitch</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_stitches" title="Cross stitches">cross stitch</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buttonhole_stitch" title="Buttonhole stitch">buttonhole or blanket stitch</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_stitch" title="Chain stitch">chain stitch</a>, and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knotted_stitch" title="Knotted stitch">knot stitch</a>.<sup about="#mwt320" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPicken1957322_31-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEPicken1957322"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957322-31"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicken1957322-31" id="mw_A"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mw_Q"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mw_g">[</span>31<span class="cite-bracket" id="mw_w">]</span></span></a></sup>

<a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing_machine" title="Sewing machine">Sewing machine</a> stitches are classified by their structure: <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_stitch" title="Chain stitch">chain stitch</a>, made with one thread; <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockstitch" title="Lockstitch">lockstitch</a>, made with two threads; and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overlock" title="Overlock">overlock</a>, made with one to four threads.</dd>

<dt id="surplice"><dfn>surplice</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surplice" title="Surplice">surplice</a> is a neckline formed by overlapping the left and right bodice pieces to form a "V".<sup about="#mwt324" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKopp_et_al.1992131_32-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"group":"","name":"FOOTNOTEKopp et al.1992131"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-FOOTNOTEKopp_et_al.1992131-32"}}'><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKopp_et_al.1992131-32" id="mwAQA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwAQE"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAQI">[</span>32<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAQM">]</span></span></a></sup></dd>

</dl>

</span><dl class="glossary " about="#mwt329" id="mwAQg">
<dt id="tailor"><dfn>tailor</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailor" title="Tailor">tailor</a> is a person who makes, repairs, or alters clothing professionally, especially <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suit_(clothing)" title="Suit (clothing)" class="mw-redirect">suits</a> and men's clothing.  Although the term dates to the thirteenth century, <i>tailor</i> took on its modern sense in the late eighteenth century, and now refers to makers of men's and women's suits, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_(clothing)" title="Coat (clothing)" class="mw-redirect">coats</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trousers" title="Trousers">trousers</a>, and similar garments, usually of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool" title="Wool">wool</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linen" title="Linen">linen</a>, or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk" title="Silk">silk</a>.</dd>

<dt id="tailored"><dfn>tailored</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailor" title="Tailor">tailor-made</a> (from the second half of the twentieth century usually simplified to <i>tailored</i>) refers to clothing made by or in the style of clothes made by a tailor, characterized by simplicity of cut and trim and fine (often hand) finishing; as a women's clothing style <i>tailored</i> is opposed to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dressmaker" title="Dressmaker">dressmaker</a>.</dd>

<dt id="thread"><dfn>thread</dfn></dt>
<dd>Thread is a fine type of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn" title="Yarn">yarn</a>.</dd>

<dt id="thimble"><dfn>thimble</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thimble" title="Thimble">thimble</a> is a protective shield worn on the finger or thumb.</dd>

<dt id="third_hand"><dfn>third hand</dfn></dt>
<dd>A <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helping_hand_(tool)" title="Helping hand (tool)">third hand</a> is a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clamp_(tool)" title="Clamp (tool)">clamp</a> that holds the fabric to be sewn.</dd>

<dt id="toile"><dfn>toile</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toile" title="Toile">Toile</a> is the British English term for a test garment.  The equivalent American English term is <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslin" title="Muslin">muslin</a>.</dd>

<dt id="trim"><dfn>trim</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trim_(sewing)" title="Trim (sewing)">Trim</a> or <i>trimming</i> in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing" title="Clothing">clothing</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_decorating" title="Home decorating" class="mw-redirect">home decorating</a> is applied ornamentation such as <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimp_(thread)" title="Gimp (thread)">gimp</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passementerie" title="Passementerie">passementerie</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbon" title="Ribbon">ribbon</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruffle_(sewing)" title="Ruffle (sewing)">ruffles</a>, or, as a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verb" title="Verb">verb</a>, to apply such ornament.</dd>

<dt id="twill_tape"><dfn>twill tape</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twill_tape" title="Twill tape">Twill tape</a> is a flat <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twill" title="Twill">twill</a>-<a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving" title="Weaving">woven</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbon" title="Ribbon">ribbon</a> of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton" title="Cotton">cotton</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linen" title="Linen">linen</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyester" title="Polyester">polyester</a>, or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool" title="Wool">wool</a>.</dd>

<dt id="yarn"><dfn>yarn</dfn></dt>
<dd><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn" title="Yarn">Yarn</a> is a long continuous length of interlocked <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber" title="Fiber">fibers</a>, suitable for use in the production of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile" title="Textile">textiles</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewing" title="Sewing">sewing</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crochet" title="Crochet">crocheting</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knitting" title="Knitting">knitting</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving" title="Weaving">weaving</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rope" title="Rope">ropemaking</a>. Yarn can be made from any number of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fiber" title="Synthetic fiber">synthetic</a> or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_fiber" title="Natural fiber">natural fibers</a>.</dd>
